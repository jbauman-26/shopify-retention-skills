# Weekly retention report

## What you need
- Shopify orders export covering **12 months or more**. Eight weeks is not enough: you can't tell a new customer from a returning one without their history.
- Klaviyo flow and campaign performance for the last 5 full weeks (optional)

## Definitions (use these exactly)
- **This week:** the most recent full Monday to Sunday that the export fully covers. Check the last order date first. If the export stops before that Sunday, use the last week it does cover and say so, or the drop you report will be a gap in the data.
- **4-week average:** the mean of the four full weeks before this week.
- **Returning-customer share:** orders this week from customers who had any earlier order, divided by all orders this week.
- **90-day repeat rate:** of customers whose first order was 90 to 180 days before the end of this week, the share who ordered again within 90 days of that first order. Compare it with the same measure four weeks earlier. It moves slowly, so report it as a trend.
- **Biggest mover:** the flow or campaign with at least 500 recipients whose revenue per recipient changed most against its own 4-week average.

## Steps
1. Calculate, for this week and the 4-week average: orders, revenue, returning-customer share, and the 90-day repeat rate.
2. If Klaviyo data was provided: table every flow and campaign sent with recipients, revenue, revenue per recipient and placed-order rate, plus email and SMS revenue as a share of store revenue. Name the biggest mover up and down with the most likely reason. Say when you can't tell.
3. If Klaviyo data was not provided: write the orders-only report and mark the email sections "not provided".
4. Leave open rate out.
5. End with three actions for this week, most valuable first, one sentence each. Each action names the number in the report it is based on.

## Output
`retention-report-YYYY-MM-DD.md`, one page: headline numbers, the table, movers, three actions.

## Rules that apply to every output

- Never invent customer quotes, results or numbers. If the data is missing, say so.
- Strip names, emails, phone numbers and order numbers from anything you quote.
- Everything you create in Klaviyo stays a draft. A person switches it on.
- Write like one person talking to one customer. Plain words, one idea per email, one link.
