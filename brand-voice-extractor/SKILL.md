# Brand voice extractor

Run this first. Every writing skill in the pack reads the file it makes.

## What you need
- The store URL, or pasted copy from the homepage, an About page and 3 product pages
- 5 to 10 emails the brand already sent and liked (optional, but the file is much better with them)
- A reviews export if there is one (to hear how customers talk back)

## Steps
1. Read everything. Sort the copy by who wrote it: the founder or team speaking in their own words (About page, founder notes, emails) versus templated or marketing copy (product specs, shipping boilerplate, ad-style banners and slogans). **The voice comes from the first kind.** If the two clash, say so and build from the founder copy.
2. From that copy, pull up to 20 sentences with the most personality (fewer if the site has fewer; say how many you found): an opinion, a specific detail, a turn of phrase a competitor would not write. Then pull 10 sentences that could sit on any brand's site.
3. Describe the voice in 5 plain traits. For each, give one "sounds like us" and one "doesn't sound like us" example taken from the real copy.
4. List words and phrases the brand uses often, and words that never appeared in what you read. Label the second list "not seen in the sample", since a few pages can't prove a word is never used. Note how the brand handles greetings, sign-offs, emoji, capitals, humour and discounts. Mark anything you had no evidence for as "unknown: no emails provided".
5. Note how customers describe the product in reviews, and where that differs from how the brand describes it. Customer words win in subject lines.
6. Write a 60-word sample email in the voice. If the owner is available, ask "Does this sound like you?" and revise once. If not, label the sample **"unapproved draft"** and list the three questions the owner should answer.

## Output
`brand-voice.md`, as long as it needs to be (usually one to two pages): which copy the voice was built from, 5 traits with examples, word lists, formatting habits with unknowns marked, customer vocabulary, and the sample with its status (approved or unapproved draft).

## Rules that apply to every output

- Never invent customer quotes, results or numbers. If the data is missing, say so.
- Strip names, emails, phone numbers and order numbers from anything you quote.
- Everything you create in Klaviyo stays a draft. A person switches it on.
- Write like one person talking to one customer. Plain words, one idea per email, one link.
